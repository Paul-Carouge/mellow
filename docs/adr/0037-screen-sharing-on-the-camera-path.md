# 0037. Screen sharing on the camera's path, before the camera interface

- Status: Accepted (owner, 2026-11-02: "je veux ça d'abord")
- Date: 2026-11-02
- Amended by: [ADR-0052](0052-h264-next-to-vp8-in-the-voice-sfu.md) (receive slots offer H.264 too where the browser decodes it), [ADR-0058](0058-sixty-frames-per-second-for-gpu-encoded-screens.md) (60 frames per second for screens the desktop encodes on the GPU)
- Builds on [ADR-0035](0035-video-in-voice-channels.md) (VP8 simulcast relayed by the built-in SFU, `STREAM`, the safeguards) and [ADR-0030](0030-voice-channels-with-an-sfu-in-the-server-binary.md).

## Context
ROADMAP stage 16.6 adds screen sharing to voice channels. The owner asked for it before the camera interface of stage 16.5: the server relays cameras since `v0.30.0`, but no client turns one on yet. A screen differs from a camera: text must stay readable (a high resolution), the owner wants it smooth (30 frames per second) at up to 1440p, and people usually show one screen at a time to the whole channel. The owner wants Vesper's capture and encoding engine used for it; a web page cannot reach it (ADR-0035, "The Vesper engine"), so the web client shares through the browser.

## Decision
- **One video source per participant, either their camera or their screen**, carried by the camera's m-line, simulcast layers, slots, layer choice, budgets and caps of ADR-0035. The server forwards it the same way; nothing is added to the media loop. Sharing a screen while the camera is on replaces the camera, as the client swaps the track on the same sender (`RTCRtpSender.replaceTrack`). Camera and screen at once (Discord allows it) is not wanted (owner, 2026-11-02: "c'est soit l'un soit l'autre"): one video source per participant stays the rule, and no second send m-line will be added.
- **Protocol (gateway `v1`, additive):** `VoiceState.self_stream: bool` and the same optional field in the `voice_state` frame. `self_stream` true means the participant's video is a screen; `self_video` and `self_stream` are never both true (a frame asking both gets the screen). Turning a screen on is checked and refused exactly as a camera (`STREAM`, 12 per channel, 25 per instance, `video_upload_mbps = 0`, `VOICE_VIDEO_REFUSED`), and the 10 s silence rule turns it off the same way. Switching between camera and screen restarts the watchers at the next keyframe.
- **Web client:** `getDisplayMedia` at the quality the person chooses (owner, 2026-11-02): **720p, 1080p or 1440p, all at 30 frames per second**, 1080p by default, remembered on the device and changeable while sharing; `contentHint = "detail"`. The shared tab's, window's or screen's **sound** is asked for too (owner, 2026-11-02, after `v0.31.0` shipped without it); the browser's picker lets the person tick it, and what it offers depends on the browser and system (Chromium: a tab's sound everywhere, the whole system's on Windows and ChromeOS; the call's own tab is left out of the picker). The three layers keep their RIDs: `h` at full size (2.5, 5 or 8 Mbit/s for the three qualities), `l` at half size up to 500 kbit/s, `q` at a quarter up to 150 kbit/s.
- **Server limits raised for 1440p:** the `h` layer may carry pictures up to 2560 × 1440 and 1.25 MB/s (10 Mbit/s) instead of ADR-0035's 1920 × 1080 and 375 kB/s, and a participant sending video up to 3 000 packets and 1.5 MB/s a second. Cameras get the same `h` limits; their own encodings stay at 1.5 Mbit/s. **Cost:** each watcher of a 1440p screen receives up to 8 Mbit/s, so ten watchers take 80 Mbit/s of the host's upload. Until the per-watcher allocation of ADR-0035 lands, `video_upload_mbps` caps it simply: each full-quality stream counts 10 Mbit/s, and once the instance forwards `video_upload_mbps / 10` of them (10 by default), the next large tiles get the half-size layer (`l`) until their watcher's next layout change. The CPU cost of several 1440p screens on the owner's server is not measured yet.
- **The screen's sound (added in 0.32.0):** offers from clients able to show video may end with one audio m-line for their shared screen's sound and 1 to `MAXIMUM_SCREEN_SOUND_RECEIVE_SLOTS` (3) audio m-lines to hear watched screens on (gateway `v1`, additive: older offers stay valid). These are audio like the voice lines, so only their place after the video lines tells them apart; the server reads their mids from the offer. Every mid is read as the WebRTC library reads it (letters and digits kept, anything else `_`, 16 characters at most) and an offer naming two lines alike is refused: a screen sound line named like the voice line would otherwise hide it from the voice budget (security audit, 2026-11-02). It forwards the sound only while the sharer's video is relayed (`STREAM`, the same caps) and the sharer holds `SPEAK` (a member a moderator silenced stays silent through their screen; their picture is still shown), only to participants of the channel who watch that screen and are not deafened, on a slot announced by `VOICE_SLOT_ASSIGNED` at its first frame and freed when they stop watching. It is not a voice: no speaking ring, the microphone's mute does not stop it, and it counts in the sender's voice packet budget, doubled while they share a screen with its sound (240 packets and 140 kB a second, else 120 and 70 kB); it is not counted in `video_upload_mbps`. The web client plays it at the sharer's volume.
  - *Amended 2026-11-03 (stream volume and sound source, docs/design/web-stream-watching.md):* the web client plays a screen's sound at its own **stream volume and mute**, per sharer and kept in the browser, apart from the sharer's voice volume and local mute, as on Discord; output volume and deafen still apply. Since browsers' pickers often offer no sound (Chromium on Linux and macOS: a tab's only; Firefox and Safari: none), the sharer may choose an **audio input** as the share's sound instead (a PipeWire or PulseAudio monitor, a virtual output for one app, Stereo Mix), opened with no echo cancellation, noise suppression or gain control and sent on the same screen sound line with `replaceTrack`; nothing changes on the wire or the server. A monitor of the whole system also carries the call's own voices unless Mellow plays on another output, which the help says.
- **Leaving a channel ends the share** (as on Discord): moving to another voice channel stops the capture and what you watched. A reconnection in the same channel keeps it and announces it again. Losing `STREAM` sends `VOICE_VIDEO_REFUSED` (`missing_permissions`), so the client stops capturing at once.
- **Offers:** clients able to show video add the camera m-line and 9 video receive m-lines to every offer, with VP8 and Opus only, as ADR-0035 says; a browser without `setCodecPreferences` joins audio only and cannot share or watch.
- **Watching:** a member sees who shares (a "Live" mark beside them in the channel) and opens the stream to watch it large; the client then lists it in `voice_video_watch` with the `large` size. Nothing is watched unless asked, so a channel where someone shares costs the others nothing until they open it.
- **Vesper:** the native desktop client (ADR-0036) will capture and encode screens with Vesper's crates and send them over the same path; that is when games at 60 frames per second and full resolution come, once the owner relicenses the crates reused.

## Consequences
- Screen sharing ships before the camera interface with almost no server work, and the camera interface later reuses the same client pieces (sender, slots, tiles).
- A participant shows their camera or their screen, never both, by the owner's choice. The screen's sound has its own volume and mute since 2026-11-03 (see the amendment above). On Windows, sharing the whole screen with the system's sound may also send what the call plays (browsers without `restrictOwnAudio`), so watchers may hear themselves or someone they muted: share a window or tab, or use headphones.
- 30 frames per second up to 1440p suits documents, code, slides and most video; games at 60 frames per second and above wait for the native client and Vesper. Above the upload budget, watchers get the half-size picture. Voice and the screen's sound have their own packet budget (240 packets and 140 kB a second, two Opus streams at their highest rate), recognised by the payload types of the voice m-line in the clear RTP header, so a burst of 1440p keyframes over 1.5 MB/s drops video only. A client marking video as voice only uses up its voice budget sooner.
