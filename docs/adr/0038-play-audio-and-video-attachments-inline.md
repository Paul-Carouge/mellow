# 0038. Play audio and video attachments inline

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-03
- Amended: 2026-11-05 (compatible brands; metadata blanked and video size read at upload)
- Amended by: [ADR-0046](0046-videos-compressed-in-the-uploaders-browser.md) (the web client compresses videos before they upload)
- Amends: [ADR-0020](0020-attachments-and-avatars-encrypted-on-disk.md)

## Context
ROADMAP item 18 (owner, #roadmap): audio files played by clicking them whatever their common format, and videos watched inside the app. ADR-0020 serves everything but checked raster pictures with `Content-Disposition: attachment` and `application/octet-stream`, so a browser can only download them. Media elements also need byte ranges: Safari plays nothing without them, and other browsers cannot seek.

## Decision
- **Sniffed types.** The upload's first bytes decide its type, as for pictures; what a client claims is never trusted. Played inline (`PLAYABLE_MEDIA_TYPES`): `video/mp4` (ISO-BMFF with an ordinary video major brand: `isom`, `iso2`, `iso4`–`iso6`, `mp41`, `mp42`, `avc1`, `M4V `, `dash`, `mmp4`; or, amended 2026-11-05, any other major brand, such as a camera's `XAVC` or `MSNV`, whose first 16 compatible brands include `isom`, `mp41` or `mp42` and no picture brand: HEIF and AVIF `mif1`, `msf1`, `heic`, `heix`, `avif`, `avis`, and Canon CR3 `crx `), `video/quicktime` (`qt  `), `video/webm` (EBML with the `webm` doc type), `audio/mp4` (`M4A `, `M4B `), `audio/mpeg` (`ID3` or an MPEG layer III frame), `audio/aac` (ADTS), `audio/ogg`, `audio/wav` (`RIFF…WAVE`), `audio/flac`. Matroska, AVI and anything else stay downloads.
- **Served inline** with that exact type, under the same headers as pictures (`nosniff`, `default-src 'none'; sandbox`, `private, no-cache`, the entity tag). None of these types is a document a browser renders with scripts.
- **Byte ranges** on `GET /api/v1/attachments/{id}` (and `/preview`): `Accept-Ranges: bytes`, one range per request (`first-last`, `first-`, `-suffix`; a last position past the end is clamped, and one answer carries at most 8 MiB: players ask for the next part themselves, and one that stops reading, paused or after the metadata, holds a download slot for a short answer instead of a whole file), answered `206` with `Content-Length` and `Content-Range`; a range starting past the end gets `416` with `bytes */total`; several ranges or a malformed header get the whole file (`200`), as RFC 9110 allows; an `If-Range` that is not the current entity tag gets the whole file. Permission check, rate limit and download slot are the same as for a whole download.
- **Throughput floor** (amendment, 2026-11-05, security audit SEC-1). The 8 MiB cap bounds what one answer holds, not for how long: a client that keeps reading, a few bytes at a time, never trips the 30 s write stall deadline and kept its download slot for hours. So every response of known length, on HTTP/1.1 and HTTP/2 alike, must be handed over whole within 30 s plus one second per 32 KiB (a full 8 MiB range: 4 min 46 s; a 25 MB file: 13 min 50 s); past that the connection is closed and the slot comes back. The deadline starts with the first byte handed to the connection (`tls::request_progress`). In addition, one reader holds at most three of the eight download slots at once (`CONCURRENT_DOWNLOADS_PER_READER`): their fourth download waits for one of their own, then answers `Busy` like a full instance, so one account can never take every slot. What remains is recorded, not closed: ceil(8 / 3) = 3 accounts can still hold every slot, each slot for up to the floor of its response, and take them again right after; on an instance with open registration that costs little, and rate limits and moderation (banning the accounts) are the answer there.
- **Metadata and size** (amended 2026-11-05). Before an MP4, QuickTime or M4A file is written, a bounded walk over its box headers (`domain::media_headers`; no decoding, no allocation per box) renames every `udta` and `meta` box, and every XMP `uuid` box (`BE7ACFCB-97A9-42E8-9C71-999491E3AFAC`, which may hold `exif:GPSLatitude`), at the top level, in `moov`, in each `trak` and in each `mdia` to `free` and zeroes its bytes in place, so `©xyz` and Apple's `com.apple.quicktime.location.ISO6709` (the place a phone filmed) never reach a reader, and offsets into `mdat` stay right. The same walk reads the first video track's `tkhd` width and height (its `hdlr` says `vide`) and swaps them when the matrix turns the picture a quarter (90° or 270°: phones' portrait videos), stored in the attachment's existing `width` and `height`, so clients size the player before it loads. A file whose boxes cannot be walked (a box size that does not fit its parent, nesting deeper than 8, more than 4 MiB of `moov` walked in all; blanked metadata is zeroed, not walked, so it does not count and large M4A cover art is accepted) is refused as malformed (`VALIDATION`), as a JPEG whose metadata cannot be found is (ADR-0033): its metadata could not be removed. At the top level only, a last box running past the end of the file (a cut recording) ends with it. `WebM` files give their first video track's `PixelWidth` and `PixelHeight`, read in their first 4 MiB (a segment whose known size runs past them, as seekable muxers write it, is read up to that point); their tags are kept (no phone writes a place there). The duration is not stored.
- **Clients** show playable attachments with a player (video with its poster frame loaded on demand, audio as a compact player) and keep the download card for the rest.

## Consequences
- Every range request decrypts the whole file (at most 25 MB by default) and takes one of the eight download slots, because files are sealed in one piece (ADR-0020). shortcut: one range request decrypts the whole file; seal in chunks (ADR-0020's own shortcut) if seeking in large videos shows on the server.
- The cap trades slots for work: a 25 MB video watched to the end takes about four range requests, each decrypting the whole file (about 100 MB decrypted per viewing).
- The throughput floor applies to every response of known length (web client assets, avatars, sounds), not only attachments: a reader slower than 32 KiB/s (256 kbit/s) loses a large download part way and retries; players simply ask for the next range. The floor is per response while the responses of one HTTP/2 connection share its bandwidth: N parallel responses need N × 32 KiB/s, and a cut closes the connection with every request on it (the client reconnects and retries). Avatars, banners, icons and sounds get the floor through the exact length of `body_keeping_permit`'s body, not a header. Responses of unknown length and upgraded `WebSocket` connections have no such deadline.
- Seeking issues more requests than one download: the 120 downloads per minute per user still apply; a player seeking wildly can meet the limit and stall for a moment; the web client keeps its player on a network error (pressing play again resumes) and shows the download card only when the browser cannot decode the file.
- MP4, QuickTime and M4A uploads lose their `udta`, `meta` and XMP boxes (amended 2026-11-05, superseding "metadata kept"), which hold `©xyz`, Apple's ISO 6709 key and XMP GPS: titles, artists and cover art of M4A songs go with the location, since `udta` and `meta` hold both. Files uploaded before keep theirs: no migration rewrites sealed files. Other audio containers (MP3's ID3, FLAC, Ogg, WAV) and `WebM` keep their tags, which phones do not fill with a place. shortcut: GPS telemetry tracks (GoPro `gpmd`, Google `camm`, DJI) keep their samples in `mdat`, and vendor `uuid` boxes (Canon's CMT) are kept; zero those samples through `stco`/`stsz` when action-camera or drone uploads matter.
- An odd but playable MP4 whose boxes break the walk's rules is now refused instead of stored; real recordings have a `moov` of a few hundred KiB and boxes three deep.
- Audio and video uploaded before this change keep `application/octet-stream` and stay downloads (their type was stored at upload); no migration rewrites them.
- No transcoding on the server (the web client compresses videos to H.264 before upload, ADR-0046): a file plays when the reader's browser can decode its codec (H.264, VP8/VP9, AV1, AAC, MP3, Opus, Vorbis, FLAC and PCM cover what phones and recorders produce). HEVC in MP4 plays in Safari and some Chrome builds only.

## Alternatives considered
- **Sniffing at download time** so old uploads play too: clients choose the player from the type in the message, which would still say `application/octet-stream`; a migration reading and decrypting every file costs more than re-uploading the few concerned.
- **Transcoding to one format on the server**: an FFmpeg dependency or a codec stack in the binary, heavy CPU on small hosts; against trivial self-hosting.
- **Streaming decryption per range** (chunked sealing): the right fix at scale, but a storage format change for all attachments; kept as the shortcut's upgrade path.
