# 0045. Audio attachments transcoded to Opus

- Status: Accepted (2026-11-05). Proposed the same day and held back because libopus needs cmake to build and the owner's Mac had none (see "Build history"); the owner then installed cmake (4.4.5) and accepted libopus as a statically linked C dependency (BSD-3-Clause).
- Date: 2026-11-05
- Supersedes the "No transcoding" consequence of [ADR-0038](0038-play-audio-and-video-attachments-inline.md) for audio only. Video stays as uploaded, and the rest of ADR-0038 (sniffed types, inline serving, byte ranges, throughput floor) is unchanged.

## Context
ADR-0038 plays audio attachments inline and transcodes nothing. It rejected transcoding on the server because it would need FFmpeg or a codec stack in the binary and heavy CPU on small hosts. For audio, that cost is small, and the files are large: a 2-minute WAV recording is 21 MB, close to the 25 MB default limit, and lossless or high-bitrate uploads fill each member's storage quota (ADR-0020). ADR-0033 already stores pictures in their smallest form that looks the same, without keeping the original. The owner asked for the same with media (audit finding media-4, 2026-11-05).

Opus at 96 kbit/s stereo is close to transparent for most listeners and most music, and 64 kbit/s is plenty for mono speech. Every browser the web client supports decodes Opus. The questions were which encoder, which container, and what the build costs.

**Encoder.** The pure-Rust Opus encoders (`rusty-opus` 1.0.2 and `opus-pure` 0.2.2, created in July and August 2026, about 70 000 downloads each) are too young for files we keep forever without an original. `unsafe-libopus` (libopus 1.3 transpiled by c2rust) has needed no C compiler since 2024, but it is unmaintained and would bring unsafe calls into our crate. Every maintained binding to libopus (`opusic-sys`, `libopus_sys`, `opus-codec`) builds the C sources with **cmake**. So we use the `opus` crate 0.4.0 (MIT or Apache-2.0) over `opusic-sys` 0.7.7 (BSD-3-Clause), which bundles libopus 1.6.1 (BSD-3-Clause), builds it with cmake and links it statically. It detects CPU features (SSE, AVX2, NEON) at run time.

**Container and Safari.** Ogg Opus (RFC 7845) plays in Chrome, Firefox and Edge. WebKit added "Ogg container support for both Opus and Vorbis audio" in Safari 18.4 (macOS 15.4, iOS and iPadOS 18.4, March 2025; [WebKit features in Safari 18.4](https://webkit.org/blog/16574/webkit-features-in-safari-18-4/)). Can I Use lists iOS Safari 18.4 and later as supported, and earlier versions as partial (Opus in CAF only). The web client targets Safari 16.4 and later (ADR-0033), so a reader on Safari 16.4 to 18.3 cannot play a transcoded file. The web client already shows its download card when the browser cannot decode an attachment (ADR-0038), and that card plays in any desktop player. Opus in MP4 or WebM might play on some of those versions, but no source documents it for `<audio>` and no such device was at hand to try, so it is not chosen on hope. Keeping AAC for Safari would need a second encoder, and FDK AAC's licence is not compatible with AGPL-3.0. Every iPhone that runs iOS 18 can update to 18.4 or later, and current releases are iOS 26 and 27. Can I Use does document Opus in CAF for those versions; serving it is weighed under "Alternatives considered" and not done: the files are stored and served as Ogg only.

## Build history (2026-11-05)
The implementation was first kept next to this record as a patch, because every maintained libopus binding builds it with cmake and the owner's Mac had no cmake: the repository's commit hook runs clippy and the tests, so every commit would have failed with `failed to run custom build command for opusic-sys`. The other ways around cmake were `unsafe-libopus` (unmaintained since 2024, no SIMD, `unsafe` calls against the workspace's `unsafe_code = "deny"`), our own `-sys` crate building about 100 C files with `cc`, or waiting for a pure-Rust encoder with a track record. The owner installed cmake instead, and the patch was applied as it was.

## Decision
- **What is transcoded.** The background attachment worker (ADR-0033) takes the sniffed types `audio/wav` and `audio/flac` always. It takes `audio/mpeg`, `audio/aac` and `audio/mp4` (M4A, M4B) only when the average bitrate of their audio is above 160 kbit/s: below that, a second lossy generation saves less than a third of the size. That bitrate counts the audio packets only, so cover art or tags cannot push a 128 kbit/s MP3 over the bar; it is known after decoding. When the container declares its length and even the whole upload averages under the bar, nothing is decoded. `audio/ogg` (already Opus or Vorbis), video, and audio with more than two channels or a sample rate outside 8 to 192 kHz stay as uploaded.
- **How.** symphonia 0.6 (pure Rust, MPL-2.0; WAV, PCM, FLAC, MP3, AAC and ISO-MP4 only) decodes one packet at a time. rubato 5 (pure Rust, MIT or Apache-2.0, sinc resampler, no FFT) resamples to 48 kHz. libopus encodes in 20 ms frames: `Application::Audio`, VBR, complexity 10, 96 kbit/s stereo or 64 kbit/s mono. The `ogg` crate (BSD-3-Clause) writes pages of about one second, with a correct pre-skip and an end granule that cuts the padding, so the file plays exactly as long as its source. A transcoded file carries no tags: titles, artists, cover art and any location go. Uploads left as they are (low-bitrate MP3 and M4A, Ogg, surround, over budget, not smaller) keep their tags (ID3, FLAC comments and pictures, WAV `LIST`, `bext` or `iXML` chunks, which recorder apps may fill with the device, the author or a place). MP4 and M4A metadata (an iPhone voice memo's `©xyz` location) is already blanked at upload. shortcut: ID3, FLAC and WAV tags of uploads left as they are are kept; extend the upload-time metadata walker to them.
- **Codecs taken.** Only the codec the sniffed container usually holds: PCM in WAV, FLAC, MP3, AAC in ADTS and M4A. symphonia's probe ignores the sniffed type, and an M4A may declare raw PCM (`lpcm`) whose frames per packet symphonia uses as its decode buffer size: a 1 KB file declaring 2^32 frames would allocate gigabytes and abort the process (security audit, 2026-11-05). Any other codec, a declared packet over 65 536 frames (FLAC's ceiling), a packet over 1 MiB or one decoding to more than 65 536 frames leaves the upload as it is.
- **Budgets.** At most 2 hours of decoded audio (`MAXIMUM_TRANSCODED_AUDIO_SECONDS`), checked against the declared length before decoding and against the decoded length after each packet: a few kilobytes of FLAC can decode to hours of silence. The encode stops as soon as its output reaches the upload's size and, after the first 30 s of audio, as soon as it reaches the audio bytes read so far: near-silent FLAC (about 100 bytes a second, against about 300 for its Opus form) would otherwise cost the whole duration budget in CPU for a few hundred kilobytes of upload, and keep the single worker busy for every other member. The comparison counts from the first sample, so a FLAC opening on 30 s or more of digital silence (a hidden-track pregap) stops there and keeps its upload: savings lost, nothing else. shortcut: no allowance ahead of the input read; add a constant one if such uploads show up. The work is therefore bounded by the input actually read, and by the 192 kHz ceiling on what one second costs to resample. At most 120 s per job (`AUDIO_TRANSCODING_TIME_BUDGET`), checked after each packet. That is an exception to the quality bar's "no time budget in background work", kept on purpose: the input is hostile, and the deadline is the backstop should a decoder spend far more per byte than measured. On a host about 8 times slower than one M3 core (a Raspberry Pi), it cuts transcodes of more than about 15 minutes of 44.1 kHz stereo; those uploads stay as they are, and the work is lost once, since an audio job is never retried. Memory therefore follows the output, which is smaller than the upload, plus a few small buffers; decoded audio is never held whole. The job takes one of the two picture-processing slots, so heavy media work stays bounded together.
- **Stored form.** The result replaces the file only when it is strictly smaller, as for pictures: a new file id, the row switched in one transaction, the old file deleted after it, and no original kept. The row takes `audio/ogg`, its size, and its name with `.opus` in place of the audio extension (`take 3.WAV` becomes `take 3.opus`). Undecodable, corrupt, over-budget or not-smaller uploads keep their file. A corrupt packet is not skipped: a decode error fails the job, and since symphonia's FLAC reader skips a frame whose checksum fails, a WAV or FLAC that decodes to fewer frames than it declares is treated as corrupt too. Only an encoder or resampler failure is logged, without the file's name.
- **Build.** cmake becomes a build prerequisite, like nasm: `SERVER_BUILD_PACKAGES` installs it in CI and in the release workflow, and the README lists it (`brew install cmake` on macOS). Self-hosters download the signed binary or the Docker image built from it, so nothing changes for them. libopus is linked statically: the binary needs no system library.

## Consequences
- Lossless audio takes 6 to 15 times less space (6.3 for the FLAC above, 14.5 for the WAVs) and high-bitrate lossy audio 2.7 to 3.8 times less, in storage, quota (ADR-0020) and download time. The per-upload limit does not grow: it is checked on the upload, before the transcode, so a WAV is still capped at about 2.5 minutes of 44.1 kHz stereo under the 25 MB default.
- The original is gone. A musician sharing a FLAC to have it mastered gets Opus back. shortcut: no "send the original" choice; add an upload flag the worker honours (a protocol change) if members ask for it.
- Readers on Safari 16.4 to 18.3 get the download card for transcoded audio instead of the player. Files uploaded before this change, and files left as they are, play as before.
- The name and type shown in a message come from the row. A client that loaded the message before the swap shows the old name and size until it reloads, as for compressed pictures.
- A job lost to a full queue (64) or a restart is not picked up again: the worker's catch-up query and its partial index (migration 0027) list picture types only. shortcut: a dropped audio job keeps its upload; add the audio types to that index with a migration if this shows up in practice. Audio uploaded before this change is never transcoded, and there is no `admin` command to do it.
- cmake is one more tool to install for anyone building Mellow from source. The GitHub-hosted Ubuntu images already ship it, and the workflow installs it explicitly.
- New C code on untrusted-input paths: none. libopus only encodes PCM that symphonia, in Rust, decoded from the upload. symphonia's decoders read untrusted bytes, within its own limits and our budgets. A security audit of the patch (2026-11-05) found no blocker: symphonia caps the tables and reads a declared size cannot inflate, a panic in the blocking task becomes a `JoinError`, no unsafe code or file name in logs, and the swap changes the file id and ETag. Its findings (the work bounded by the input read, the bitrate of the audio alone, the time budget recorded as an exception, tests for FLAC, M4A, a corrupt frame and a stream changing layout) are in the implementation and in this record.
- New crates: `opus`, `opusic-sys`, `ogg`, `rubato` (with `audioadapter`, `audioadapter-buffers`, `audioadapter-sample`, `num-complex`, `windowfunctions`, `visibility`), `symphonia` and its parts (with `extended`, `regex-lite`, `audio-codec-algorithms`). All pass `cargo deny`. BSD-3-Clause asks that binary distributions reproduce libopus's copyright notice: it is in `web/public/third-party-notices.txt`, which the server binary embeds and serves at `/third-party-notices.txt`, linked from Settings, About (ADR-0031). The other C libraries the server already bundles (libwebp, SQLCipher, OpenSSL, aws-lc) are not listed there yet. shortcut: notices are kept by hand; generate the Rust crates' part with a tool such as cargo-about and add those C libraries if the list grows.

## Alternatives considered
- **No transcoding** (ADR-0038): the status quo. Simple, but a WAV costs 15 times its Opus form in storage, quota and download time.
- **A pure-Rust Opus encoder**: no C toolchain, but the crates are three to four months old, and the files are kept without an original. Revisit once one has a track record. Swapping the encoder would only change `OggOpusEncoder`.
- **`unsafe-libopus`** (c2rust transpile): no cmake, but unmaintained since 2024, libopus 1.3 without SIMD, and unsafe calls in our crate.
- **AAC in MP4 for Safari**: plays on every Safari, but there is no AGPL-compatible encoder of comparable quality, and two outputs per upload would double the work.
- **Opus in CAF for Safari 16.4 to 18.3**: the one container Can I Use documents for those versions. Storing a second CAF copy doubles the work and the storage this record sets out to save. Remuxing the stored Opus packets into CAF on download (no re-encode) avoids that, but it adds a second muxer, a response chosen by user agent (`Vary: User-Agent`, a second ETag, byte ranges over a generated body) on a route that today serves stored bytes as they are, for browser versions every device running them can leave with a free update. Not done; those readers get the download card, and this is the alternative to revisit if they complain.
- **Opus in WebM or MP4**: not documented to play on the Safari versions that lack Ogg, and not tried on them; Ogg is the format RFC 7845 defines and every other browser plays.
- **Transcoding in the browser before upload**: there is no WebCodecs Opus encoder in Safari, and a Wasm libopus would add hundreds of kilobytes to the client. The server already has a worker and the budgets.
- **FFmpeg on the host**: breaks the one-binary promise.
