# 0033. Visually lossless image compression

- Status: Accepted (owner's decision, 2026-11-01)
- Date: 2026-11-02
- Supersedes [ADR-0021](0021-compress-images-losslessly-after-upload.md). What it decided and this ADR does not change still holds: attachments are accepted first and compressed after, JPEG metadata is stripped before the upload is answered, decoding is pure Rust within the same limits, animated avatars, banners, guild icons and emoji are free for everyone with a still first frame, and the protocol and serving rules stay.

## Context
On 2026-11-01 the owner asked that every picture the server stores be as small as possible with no visible loss of quality, and that attachment originals not be kept. That covers avatars, banners, guild and channel icons, custom emoji, picture attachments and animated GIFs. Colours must never visibly change, including for photos tagged with a wide-gamut colour profile (Display P3 iPhone photos). ADR-0021 chose lossless encoding only: lossless WebP for profile pictures, icons and emoji, `oxipng` for PNG attachments, JPEG kept as uploaded (metadata stripped), and GIF converted to lossless animated WebP. It rejected lossy WebP and AVIF because they "visibly change pictures". A measured spike shows that this is not true once the quality is chosen per picture against a perceptual metric.

The spike used 25 pictures: 9 Kodak photos (768 × 512); 5 large public-domain photos and paintings from Wikimedia Commons (1 920 to 3 864 px, 2.5 to 15 MP); 3 screenshots of text and UI (1 280 × 900 to 2 880 × 1 800); a vector illustration; 3 transparent logos (1 024²); and 3 animated GIFs (a dithered photo pan, a fractal, and a flat 64-colour test pattern). Every picture was also run at the sizes Mellow stores. Quality was measured with SSIMULACRA2 (`ssimulacra2` crate). Its scale reads: 80 means "not noticeable side by side", 85 "not noticeable in an in-place comparison at 1:1", and 90 "not noticeable in a flicker test at 1:1". For each encoder, a binary search found the lowest quality that kept each score. Timings come from one core of an Apple M3 (fanless, so numbers vary by up to 2×).

Results of the spike, for attachments, keeping the smallest candidate that passes:
- At a score of 85, the 25 attachments need **18.7 MiB** with AVIF or lossless WebP. Lossy WebP or lossless WebP needs 20.4 MiB. ADR-0021's pipeline needs about 32.9 MiB, and the uploads total 36.4 MiB. At 90 the totals are 22.8 MiB with AVIF and 30.4 MiB with lossy WebP. Lossy WebP cannot reach 90 on most photos even at quality 100, because it always subsamples colour (4:2:0).
- AVIF is the smallest for 17 of 25 pictures, 25–35 % below lossy WebP on photos and 40–60 % below on transparent logos. **Lossless WebP is the smallest for screenshots and flat graphics**: a 1 920 × 1 080 chat screenshot takes 105 KB lossless, 122 KB as lossy WebP and 155 KB as AVIF. For 1 of the 5 JPEG uploads, re-encoding cannot beat the uploaded file.
- Animated: a dithered photo GIF of 3.2 MB becomes 1.6 MB as lossy animated WebP at 85, against 2.6 MB lossless. Flat or fractal GIFs stay smallest as lossless WebP, because lossy never reaches 85 on them.
- Cost: an AVIF encode at speed 8 takes about 0.11 s for a 256² avatar, 0.6 s for 0.4 MP, 1.1 s for a 960 px banner, 4–6 s for 5 MP and 20–50 s for 15 MP. It peaks at about 720 MB of memory for 15 MP. Speed 6 saves only 1–3 % of bytes over speed 8 for 1.5–2× the time; speed 10 loses 10–60 %. SSIMULACRA2 on a whole 15 MP picture takes 14 s and 2.7 GB; on 8 tiles of 512² it takes about 0.5 s, and the chosen quality stays within ±2 steps of the whole-picture search.
- Browsers: the web client targets ES2022, which means Safari 16.4 or later. Still AVIF works in Chrome 85+, Firefox 93+ and Safari 16.4+, colour signalling (CICP) included. WebP, including animated WebP, works everywhere the client runs.

## Decision
- **Quality target: SSIMULACRA2 ≥ 85 against the decoded upload**, after resizing to the stored size. The score is the worst of up to 8 tiles of 512 × 512 spread over the picture (the whole picture when it holds no more). Transparent pictures are scored over the chat sheet (`color.surface.chat`) and over white, and the worse score counts. For an animation, up to 12 evenly spaced frames are scored against the frame shown at the same time, and the worst counts. A lossless result always passes. Pictures under 8 pixels a side cannot be scored, so only lossless results pass for them.
- **Candidates and choice.** The server keeps the smallest candidate that passes:
  - Still pictures: **AVIF** at speed 8, one thread per job, 10-bit 4:4:4, encoded with `rav1e` directly and wrapped by `avif-serialize`, with the settings `ravif` 0.13 uses (the ones the spike measured). `ravif` itself is not used: it cannot signal Display P3 and does not return the encoder's reconstruction. That reconstruction is exactly what a decoder shows, so it is what gets scored, and no AV1 decoder is needed. **Lossless WebP** is also tried for PNG, GIF and WebP sources (screenshots, logos, graphics), never for JPEG photos, where it is never smaller. For a JPEG attachment, the **uploaded JPEG with its metadata stripped** stays when nothing smaller passes.
  - Animations (GIF and animated WebP): lossy animated WebP (`use_sharp_yuv`, lossless alpha) and lossless animated WebP, both from `libwebp` through `webp-animation`. Pure Rust has no animated AVIF encoder. The still first frame of an animated avatar, banner, guild icon or emoji is encoded like any still picture.
  - An animated PNG attachment is left as uploaded: decoding it reads only its first frame.
- **Quality search, bounded.** A binary search over quality 60–95, starting at 88 for AVIF and 90 for lossy WebP (the medians measured for a score of 85), with at most 4 encodes per format. The best passing result so far is kept, so stopping early still leaves a valid picture. No encode starts after a time budget: 60 s per attachment job, 10 s for pictures processed while uploading (whose request gives up after 20 s). `rav1e` cannot be interrupted, so an encode already running finishes.
- **Colour.** The embedded ICC profile of a JPEG or PNG is read with `moxcms` (already in the tree through `image`):
  - No profile, an unreadable one, or one that maps every colour of a sampled grid to within 2 steps of sRGB: the picture is sRGB and stored as it is, as browsers show it.
  - A grey or CMYK profile (print exports): the decoder turns the pixels into RGB without it, while browsers show the file through it, so the decoded pixels are not what people see. An attachment with such a profile keeps its file (a JPEG stays as uploaded, metadata stripped and profile kept, as under ADR-0021). An avatar, banner, icon or emoji, which must be re-encoded, is written from the decoded pixels as before.
  - A profile within 2 steps of Display P3 (iPhone photos): the pixels stay as they are and the AVIF signals Display P3 primaries with sRGB transfer (CICP `nclx` 12/13/6, full range).
  - Any other RGB profile (Adobe RGB, calibrated display profiles): the pixels are converted to Display P3 with a colour-managed transform and stored the same way. An attachment keeps its file when one of its pixels does not come back within 2 steps through Display P3 and back (colours outside Display P3, such as the purest Adobe RGB greens), or when the profile cannot be inverted to check it. An avatar or icon is converted anyway, the out-of-gamut colours clipped to Display P3.
  - **Scoring is done in the stored colour space**: SSIMULACRA2 reads both the reference (the stored pixels before encoding) and the candidate with the primaries they are stored in, BT.709 for sRGB and Display P3 for wide-gamut pictures. Reading Display P3 pixels as sRGB would pull their saturated colours into the sRGB gamut and weigh their errors too low.
  - Lossless WebP is written without a profile, so it is a candidate for a wide-gamut picture only in its sRGB form, when every pixel comes back within 2 steps through sRGB and back. That covers most screenshots macOS tags with Display P3 or its display's profile, whose interface colours are sRGB ones. When nothing passes for a picture that must be stored (an avatar, an icon), that lossless WebP is stored when it exists, otherwise AVIF at quality 100; an attachment keeps its file. A still WebP's own profile is not read: its decoder sizes that chunk without a limit.
- **Stored sizes**: three times the largest CSS size at which each picture is shown, for high-density screens.
  - Avatars: **256 × 256** (largest display 80 px in the profile popover), down from 512.
  - Banners: **at most 960 × 384** (300 × 120 card), down from 1 920 px wide. The card covers its box, so a banner taller than 5:2 only ever shows its middle band; that band is what is stored. This also bounds the work of a banner upload to 0.37 MP.
  - Guild icons: 256 (80 px icon picker, 48 px rail), unchanged.
  - Channel icons: 96, unchanged.
  - Emoji: 128 (48 px jumbo), unchanged.
  - Attachments: kept at full resolution, because the link opens the stored file at full size. Only attachments up to **16 MP** a frame are compressed; larger ones (up to the existing 8 192 px and 24 MP decode limits) keep their file. Peak memory of one job, measured in release: 0.67 GB for a 12.2 MP JPEG photo, 0.99 GB for the same picture as PNG (lossless WebP is tried too), 0.84 GB at 16 MP as JPEG and 1.23 GB as PNG, 1.73 GB at 24 MP. 16 MP keeps the 13–15 MP photos of the measured corpus compressed; 24 MP phone photos keep their stripped JPEG.
  - *Amended 2026-11-02 (attachment previews):* the message list shows a picture attachment in a box of at most 400 × 300 CSS px, so a picture larger than **800 × 600** (twice that box, for high-density screens) also gets a **preview** fitted within it. Twice rather than three times: the preview gives way to the full file as soon as the picture is opened. The background job makes it from the upload right after the stored form (no AV1 decoder reads the stored AVIF back), with the same candidates, quality search and colour rules, scored against the fitted picture, and its own 20 s budget so a large picture that used all of its 60 s still gets one. Animations get an animated preview. The preview is kept only when smaller than the stored file. It is its own sealed file in the attachment store, recorded in three columns of the attachment's row (migration 0028), never replaced, and swept with its row by the existing purge. `GET /api/v1/attachments/{id}/preview` serves it with the attachment route's permission check, headers and `ETag` scheme (the preview's own file id and size), and serves the attachment itself when there is no preview: the picture already fits, nothing smaller passed, its colours would change, or it was uploaded before migration 0028. Previews do not count against the uploader's quota: the server makes them, and each is smaller than the file it stands for. The web client loads previews in the message list and the link opens the full file.
- **Orientation and metadata.** EXIF orientation is applied to the pixels before encoding, for attachments too, so the size recorded at upload stays true. AVIF and WebP are written from pixels, so no metadata survives (no EXIF, XMP or GPS position).
- **Where it runs.** Avatars, banners, icons and emoji are processed while uploading, as before. Attachments are accepted first and compressed after; the background worker now takes PNG, JPEG, GIF and WebP, and replaces the stored file, its type and its name's extension with the result (`photo.jpg` becomes `photo.avif`). No original is kept. At most two pictures are processed at once, server-wide. Pictures over 1 MP can only be attachments (every fitted avatar, banner and icon is smaller, which a compile-time check holds), and the single worker runs one job at a time, so only one large job ever runs: peak memory stays near 1.3 GB in the worst case (a 16 MP PNG attachment and one upload). The worker records its attempt **before** processing a picture, so a picture that kills the process (out of memory) is never tried again at the next start.
- **Types.** Profile, guild and emoji pictures are served with the type read from their stored bytes (`image/avif` or `image/webp`), stills included, instead of a fixed `image/webp`. No content-type column is added: the server wrote those bytes itself, a picture and its still may differ in type, and reading the type back is what attachment downloads already do. This departs from the original brief, which asked for a content-type column and its migration; the owner has to accept the departure before this ADR's implementation is merged, or the column is added. `image/avif` joins the types served inline and shown by the web client; uploads of avatars, icons and emoji stay limited to PNG, JPEG, GIF and WebP, the formats the server decodes.
- **Existing pictures are not touched.** Migration 0027 marks every picture attachment stored before it as already tried, so an update never recompresses history. Recompressing existing pictures needs its own administrator command and the owner's confirmation; it is not part of this decision.

## Consequences
- Measured with the shipped pipeline (release build, Apple M3, `image_corpus_sizes`) on the spike's 25 pictures: the attachments, uploaded at 36.4 MiB (JPEGs stripped), are stored in **17.4 MiB**, against about 32.9 MiB with ADR-0021's pipeline. AVIF wins for 18 pictures, lossless WebP for the 3 screenshots, and lossy or lossless animated WebP for the 3 GIFs; even the JPEG the spike could not beat comes out 4 % smaller. Photo avatars take 8–22 KB at 256 px; photo banners of at most 960 × 384 take 25–170 KB (50–520 KB before banners kept only the band the card shows). A Display P3 copy of a photo is stored as a P3-tagged AVIF 26 % below its JPEG. An Adobe RGB copy of a Kodak picture keeps its PNG: its brightest reds lie outside Display P3 (up to 14 steps off after the round trip), so converting it would change them. Display P3 copies of the chat and code screenshots are stored as lossless WebP of 107 KB each (sRGB form), where they were 184 and 143 KB of AVIF. A CMYK JPEG keeps its file. The slowest attachment, a 13.6 MP painting, took 45 s; Kodak photos about 1.3 s each.
- The reconstruction scored matches what macOS ImageIO decodes from the same AVIF files within one 8-bit step (mean difference 0.04–0.06), on a 15 MP photo, a 0.4 MP photo and a vector illustration.
- Animated avatars of flat or fractal GIFs can be larger than before: resizing them to 256 px blends their few colours into many, which lossless WebP compresses badly and lossy WebP cannot keep at a score of 85 (a 480 × 270 flat test pattern: 212 KB as an attachment, 647 KB as an avatar).
- New dependencies: `rav1e` (BSD-2-Clause) with about 45 more crates, `avif-serialize` (BSD-3-Clause) and `ssimulacra2` (BSD-2-Clause, with `yuvxyb`); `moxcms` was already in the tree. `oxipng` is removed. A clean release build takes 20–35 s longer and the stripped binary grows by about 1.9 MB (measured in the spike). Tests build `rav1e`, `ssimulacra2` and `yuvxyb` optimised even in debug, or they would be tens of times slower.
- `rav1e`'s assembly makes AVIF encoding 25–35 % faster. On aarch64 it is compiled by `cc`. On x86-64 it needs `nasm` on the build machine, so CI and the release workflow install it. Nothing is needed at run time: the binary stays a single static file.
- `cargo deny` limits its graph to the targets Mellow builds for. `rav1e`'s `libfuzzer-sys` (NCSA licence) is a `cfg(fuzzing)`-only dependency that is never compiled, so it leaves the graph without a licence exception.
- A large attachment can keep one core busy for one to several minutes on a small host: the time budget only stops the search between encodes. The queue keeps uploads instant, but other attachments wait their turn.
- People downloading an attachment get AVIF or WebP, not the file they sent. macOS 13+, iOS 16+, Windows 11 and every supported browser open both.
- A JPEG's stripped original is kept when nothing smaller passes, so already compressed photos never grow, and generation loss stays within a score of 85 against what was uploaded.
- Previews, measured the same way on a 26-picture copy of the corpus (40.0 MB uploaded, 19.7 MB stored): the message list loads **6.0 MB** instead of 19.7 MB. A 3 900 px JPEG stored as 1.43 MB of AVIF shows from an 86 KB preview, a 15.4 MB painting stored as 5.5 MB from 108 KB, and the 1 920 px chat screenshot from 40 KB instead of 108 KB. Kodak photos (768 × 512) and the GIFs already fit, so they show their stored file. A preview takes 0.8–3.9 s of one core after the stored form.
- An `ETag` still identifies a stored attachment by its size: the worker replaces a file only with a strictly smaller one (ADR-0020's amendment).

## Alternatives considered
- **Keep lossless only (ADR-0021)**: no visible loss either, but about 1.75 times the space for attachments and 4–15 times for photo avatars and banners. That goes against "as small as possible".
- **Lossy WebP only**: 10–20 times faster than AVIF, but 10–35 % larger, and it cannot reach a score of 90 on most photos.
- **AVIF only, without lossless WebP**: up to 50 % larger on screenshots and flat graphics, which are a large share of what a chat stores.
- **Fixed quality per kind, without a metric**: simpler and faster. But measured quality needs vary from 63 to 92 for AVIF at a score of 85, so one setting either wastes space on graphics or visibly damages detailed photos.
- **`ravif` plus an AV1 decoder (`rav1d`) to score**: a second large codec, mostly ported C with much `unsafe`, to rebuild pixels the encoder already has.
- **Converting every wide-gamut picture to sRGB**: lossless WebP would stay a candidate, but saturated Display P3 colours fall outside sRGB and would be clipped, which the owner ruled out. Keeping such photos uncompressed was the draft's fallback; it is not needed since AVIF signals Display P3.
- **An ICC profile inside the AVIF**: carries any colour space exactly, but `avif-serialize` writes only CICP, and Display P3 covers the gamut of nearly every real photo.
- **A content-type column for profile and guild pictures**: needs a migration and a second column for still frames, for information the stored bytes already hold.
- **libavif or libaom (C)**: animated AVIF and faster encoders, but they need `cmake` and a C/C++ build in a single static binary, and they increase the unsafe surface.
- **JPEG XL**: no browser support in Chrome or Firefox by default.
- **A threshold of 90 (flicker test)**: about 22 % more space for attachments and about 50 % more for banners, against a difference no one sees in normal use. It stays a one-constant change (`quality::MINIMUM_SCORE`).
- **The whole-picture metric**: 14 s and 2.7 GB for a 15 MP photo. Tiling gives the same choice within ±2 quality steps for about 3 % of the cost.
