# 0046. Videos compressed in the uploader's browser

- Status: Accepted (2026-11-05, the owner approved the Content-Security-Policy change)
- Date: 2026-11-05
- Amends: [ADR-0038](0038-play-audio-and-video-attachments-inline.md) (videos are no longer always stored as uploaded), [ADR-0019](0019-web-client-served-by-the-server-binary.md) (the policy's `worker-src`)

## Context
The owner wants videos "as small as possible at near-original quality". Phones record 1080p or 4K H.264 or HEVC at 15 to 50 Mbit/s, so a 10 s clip is 20 to 60 MB and a minute does not fit the instance's 25 MiB default limit. ADR-0038 stores and serves videos as uploaded, with the `moov` box often after `mdat` (players then fetch the end first). Transcoding on the server was rejected there and stays rejected: no pure-Rust H.264 decoder, an FFmpeg dependency breaks the one-binary promise, and minutes of CPU per clip on small hosts. The uploader's browser has hardware encoders behind WebCodecs (`VideoEncoder`), and the composer already uploads each file at once into its tray (ADR-0020) with an abortable request.

## Decision
- **Where.** The web client compresses a video (`file.type` is `video/*`) before it uploads, in a dedicated module Worker (`web/src/lib/video-compression.worker.ts`), so decoding, scaling, encoding and muxing never touch the main thread. Vite builds the Worker and its library into their own chunk, loaded only when a video is attached. A browser without `Worker` or `VideoEncoder` (Firefox on Android, older Safari) sends the original without loading anything.
- **Library: mediabunny 1.65.2** (MPL-2.0, by Vanilagy and contributors; 1.65.2 published 2026-11-04, releases every few weeks; zero runtime dependencies, its two dependencies are type packages). It reads MP4, QuickTime, WebM and Matroska, drives WebCodecs, and writes a faststart MP4. The Worker chunk is 461 kB minified, 93 kB Brotli. MPL-2.0 is file-level copyleft and compatible with the AGPL-3.0: the package is bundled unmodified, its per-file licence headers stay in the chunk, and its notice and licence are in `third-party-notices.txt`. It replaced `mp4-muxer` and `webm-muxer`, which its author deprecated in its favour. Pinned exactly in `web/package-lock.json`; `npm audit` reports nothing new.
- **Output: H.264 video, AAC or Opus audio, in a faststart MP4** (`fastStart: "in-memory"`, `ftyp`, `moov`, `mdat`), without the source's metadata tags (`tags: {}`: no location, device or dates; the server's ADR-0038 walk stays as the boundary check). H.264 is the only video codec every reader's browser decodes: AV1 is about half the size at equal quality (SVT-AV1 at 2 Mbit/s matched H.264 at 4 Mbit/s on the test clips) but Safari plays it only with AV1 hardware (iPhone 15 Pro, M3 and later), so many readers would get the download card; VP9 in realtime mode overshot its rate by 90 % and gains little over hardware H.264. Revisit AV1 when the readers' floor moves. Audio: AAC or Opus already in the source is copied; other audio is encoded to AAC at 128 kbit/s, or Opus at 96 kbit/s where the browser has no AAC encoder (Chrome on Linux).
- **Size and rate.** Never upscaled. Scaled down to fit a 1920 × 1080 box in the video's own orientation (portrait clips stay 1080 wide; rotation is baked into the frames). Frame rate kept up to 60, brought to 60 above. Key frame every 2 s.
- **Bits per pixel: 0.1** (`TARGET_BITS_PER_PIXEL`): about 6.2 Mbit/s at 1080p30, 12.4 Mbit/s at 1080p60, 2.8 Mbit/s at 720p30, in variable bitrate (static content, such as screen recordings, comes out smaller). Chosen on the measurements below: 0.07 (the research's first figure, measured with FFmpeg's VideoToolbox) gave only VMAF 86 and 71 with Chrome's own encoder; 0.1 gives 90 and 76 at about a quarter of the original size.
- **The instance's limit** (`Ready.maximum_attachment_bytes`). When a clip at the target would not fit, the bitrate is lowered to fit 90 % of the limit, down to a floor of 0.05 bits per pixel (`MINIMUM_BITS_PER_PIXEL`); below the floor the original is kept (and refused by the size check if it is too large) rather than sending something smeared. A source over the limit is never only remuxed, and any output that fits is sent when the original does not, whatever it saved. The size check runs on the compressed file, before any byte is sent.
- **Codecs not every browser plays** (HEVC, VP9, AV1, anything but H.264) are always transcoded when the browser decodes them, even near the target, so readers on Firefox and most Chrome builds get a playable file instead of the download card. Their bitrate is at most 1.5 times the source's total rate (`COMPATIBILITY_BITRATE_RATIO`: H.264 needs about half again HEVC's bits for the same picture), so a small clip is not inflated to the target, and the result is sent when it fits the limit, even when it is a little larger than the original: compatibility, not size, is the gain.
- **The original is kept** when: the browser cannot decode the video (HEVC on most non-Apple systems), the duration is unknown, the source is HDR (`pq` or `hlg` transfer: tone mapping to SDR H.264 washes it out), the browser has no H.264 encoder, the frame rate is unknown, the audio can be neither copied nor encoded, a track would be dropped, the conversion fails (logged, never lost), or an H.264 transcode is not at least 20 % smaller (unless the original is over the limit). A source already near the target (at most 1.25 times the target total rate, no scaling, at most 60 fps, within the limit) in H.264 is only remuxed (packets copied: milliseconds), and that remux is kept when it is not larger, for the faststart and the dropped tags.
- **Channel.** Each tray item keeps the channel it was attached in and uploads there, whatever channel is open when its compression ends.
- **Tray.** The Worker posts progress once per whole percent, not per frame. The item says "Compression… 42 %" / "Compressing… 42%" with the preview dimmed as while uploading, and a "Envoyer l'original" / "Send the original" link (the Retry link's style, labelled "Send the original of clip.mov") that stops compression and uploads the file as it is. The item's Remove button cancels: it stops the Worker and nothing is uploaded. Send waits for compressing files as for uploading ones. No new screen or token.
- **Content-Security-Policy: `worker-src 'self'`** instead of `'none'`. Browsers load a worker script only when it is served with a JavaScript type; on this origin only the client's own hashed assets are, since uploads are sniffed to media types or `application/octet-stream` and served with `nosniff` (ADR-0020, ADR-0038), so an upload still cannot become a Worker or a service worker. Approved by the owner on 2026-11-05 and applied in `web_client.rs` (both policies and their tests).

## Consequences
- Most phone videos upload four times smaller and start playing at once (faststart); a minute of 1080p fits the 25 MiB default where it did not.
- The uploader's device does the work: about 1.6 s for 10 s of 1080p with hardware encoding, several times longer in software, and battery on phones. "Send the original" and Remove are always there.
- The output is a second lossy generation: VMAF 76 to 90 at the target, so fine detail in high-motion clips softens. Readers who need the exact file use "Send the original".
- iPhone HEVC that is SDR becomes H.264 and plays in every browser, on browsers that can decode HEVC (Safari, Chrome on macOS and Windows with hardware support); elsewhere it stays HEVC; HDR iPhone clips (the default since iPhone 12) stay as they are, HEVC that Firefox and most Chrome builds cannot play. shortcut: HDR sources are kept; tone-map to SDR (or keep HEVC when every reader's browser decodes it) when HDR uploads are common.
- `BufferTarget` holds the output in the Worker's memory: at most the instance's limit plus the encoder's buffers.
- The server is unchanged: it still checks size, type and metadata of whatever arrives, compressed or not.
- shortcut: the 0.1 bits-per-pixel target was measured on two reference clips with Chrome on macOS only; measure real phone clips and Chrome with OpenH264 on Linux before treating it as settled.

## Alternatives considered
- **Transcoding on the server**: rejected by ADR-0038 (FFmpeg or a codec stack in the binary, CPU on small hosts).
- **FFmpeg compiled to WebAssembly**: about 30 MB of WebAssembly, single-threaded software x264, and LGPL/GPL build questions.
- **AV1 or VP9 output**: smaller (AV1) but not playable in every reader's Safari; VP9 overshoots in realtime mode. See the output decision.
- **A fixed quality preset (`QUALITY_HIGH`)** instead of bits per pixel: mediabunny's presets are tuned for its own targets and give no control tied to the instance's limit.
- **Compressing on the main thread**: simpler, but decoding and encoding callbacks would compete with typing and scrolling.

## Amendment (2026-11-13): the desktop client compresses with the system's media stack
The GPUI desktop (ADR-0051) compresses videos before upload with the same rules, ported line for line to `mellow-media::video_compression` (the 1080p box, 0.1 and 0.05 bits per pixel, the 1.25 remux ratio, the 1.5 compatibility ratio, the 20 % saving, 90 % of the instance's limit, key frames every 2 s, AAC 128 kbit/s or Opus 96 kbit/s, the MP4 name) with the web's tests, so the instance receives the same kind of file from both clients: H.264 with AAC or Opus in a faststart MP4 without the source's tags, rotation baked into the frames. Files are taken by extension where a browser would type them `video/*`; pictures and audio still go as they are (ADR-0033, ADR-0045).
- **macOS**: AVFoundation. An `AVAssetReader` decodes through a video composition (rotation applied, at most 60 frames a second), an `AVAssetWriter` encodes H.264 with VideoToolbox and AAC and writes with `shouldOptimizeForNetworkUse`. A remux copies the packets with the track's transform. AVFoundation reads MP4 and QuickTime only, so WebM and Matroska keep their original on macOS (Chrome on macOS would compress them), and Opus in a source is encoded to AAC (the writer does not pass Opus through into MP4).
- **Linux**: the system FFmpeg the desktop already links (ADR-0051's amendment), now also through **libavformat** (reading the file, writing the MP4 with `movflags=+faststart`): decoders with an 8K pixel limit, a filter graph (`transpose`, `scale`, `setsar`, `fps`, then `format=nv12,hwupload` or `format=yuv420p`), `h264_vaapi` when a VA-API device opens, else `libx264` or `libopenh264` when the FFmpeg has them, retried in that order when an encoder fails; AAC with FFmpeg's own encoder, else `libopus`. The release's pinned LGPL FFmpeg must therefore enable, besides the h264 decoder and `h264_vaapi`: the `mov` and `matroska` demuxers, the `file` protocol, the `mp4` muxer, the decoders of what phones and cameras record (h264, hevc, vp8, vp9, av1, mpeg4, aac, opus, pcm, mp3), the `aac` encoder, and the `transpose`, `scale`, `setsar`, `fps`, `format`, `hwupload`, `aresample`, `aformat` and buffer filters. Without a software H.264 encoder (the release has none) a machine without VA-API keeps the original, as a browser without an H.264 encoder does. Only the `mov` and `matroska` demuxers may open a file (`format_whitelist`), as the web reads MP4, QuickTime, Matroska and WebM only: AVI, MPEG-TS and the rest keep their original on both clients. The release FFmpeg is configured with `--disable-protocols --enable-protocol=file` and without libxml2; the app also sets `protocol_whitelist=file`, since distributions' FFmpeg have both.
- **Windows** sends the original until Media Foundation comes with its own change.
- **Interface**: as the web's tray: "Compressing… 42%" with the preview dimmed and the "Send the original" link; Remove stops the encoder and uploads nothing; Send waits. The encoder runs on a thread of its own and checks a cancel flag at every sample; progress reaches the interface once per whole percent through a bounded queue. The compressed file is held in memory (at most the original's size or the instance's limit) and kept for Retry.
- **Untrusted input**: the file is the uploader's own, picked by them; FFmpeg's and AVFoundation's demuxers and decoders read it in the app's process, as players do. The decoded picture size is capped before allocation on Linux. Nothing outside the picked file is read: on Linux only the `file` protocol and the two demuxers above (a DASH or HLS manifest named `.mp4` would otherwise fetch its segments from the network or from other local files), on macOS `AVURLAssetReferenceRestrictionsKey` is `ForbidAll` (a reference movie would otherwise pull other local media into the upload). A file whose samples run past 1.5 times its stated duration (plus a second) stops the encode, so a lying duration cannot grow the output; the cancel flag also interrupts FFmpeg's blocking reads.
