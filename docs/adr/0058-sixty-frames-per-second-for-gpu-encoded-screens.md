# 0058. Sixty frames per second for GPU-encoded screens

- Status: Accepted (lead's decision for the owner, 2026-11-14, wave 10)
- Date: 2026-11-14
- Amends: [ADR-0037](0037-screen-sharing-on-the-camera-path.md) ("720p, 1080p or 1440p, all at 30 frames per second") for the desktop client's GPU path
- Builds on: [ADR-0054](0054-native-screen-sharing-on-the-desktop.md) (H.264 through VA-API from DMA-BUFs on Linux), [ADR-0052](0052-h264-next-to-vp8-in-the-voice-sfu.md)

## Context
ADR-0037 shares every screen at 30 frames per second, a browser's limit with a software VP8 encoder, and left "games at 60 frames per second" for the native client. The desktop client now encodes a Wayland share on the GPU (ADR-0054). Measured on the owner's machine (RX 9070 XT, Mesa 26.2, 2026-11-14), with the settings Mellow uses (Constrained Baseline, no B-frames, constant bit rate, half-second buffer, one picture in flight):
- The H.264 encoder takes 1440p at 253 pictures a second through FFmpeg (synthetic pictures uploaded from the CPU; the portal's DMA-BUFs skip that upload). Vesper's measured encoder throughput (620 Mpx/s for H.264 at its quality level) puts a 1440p60 top layer and the two lower layers at about 40 % of the GPU's video encoder.
- AMD's "constant" rate overshoots what it is asked by about 17 %. Asked 8 Mbit/s, 1440p at 30 gave 9.4 on average and 10.4 at worst over one second; at 60, 9.3 and 10.1. Asked 9 Mbit/s at 1440p60, it gave 10.5 and 11.3, which with the lower layers, RTX and headers passes the server's 1.5 MB/s (12 Mbit/s) per sender, so packets would be dropped.
- libvpx (VP8 on X11, macOS and Windows, and on Linux without VA-API) costs one to two cores at 1440p30 (ADR-0051); 60 was not measured and would double it.

## Decision
- **The GPU path follows the source's rate, 30 or 60 frames per second.** On Linux with VA-API the portal is asked for up to 60 pictures a second; it sends a new picture only when the screen changes. The GPU's constant rate gives each picture a fixed share of the second's bits at the rate it was opened with, so the top layer (`h`) is encoded at the rate the source really has:
  - The source's new pictures are counted over the last second. The encoder moves to 60 once the source held 45 or more a second for one second, and back to 30 once it held 35 or fewer for two seconds; between the two thresholds it stays where it is, so a source near 40 never makes it switch back and forth.
  - VA-API (through FFmpeg) cannot change the rate of an open encoder: a switch reopens the three layers' encoders, so each sends a keyframe (79 kB at 1440p60, 13 kB for `l`, 4 kB for `q`, measured), at most once a second up and once in two seconds down. Keyframes the server asked for while they reopened are dropped: those keyframes answer them. Encoders that cannot open at the new rate leave the share at its current rate (a warning) and the switch is tried again only once the source's rate held for a whole window again; only encoders for a new source (size or quality) that cannot open end the share.
  - The top layer takes pictures through a token bucket refilled at the encoder's rate and holding one and a half tokens at most: on average never more pictures than the rate its constant bit rate was budgeted for. The newest picture waits for its token and replaces any older one waiting, which goes back to the compositor; the capture hands pictures over through a slot where the newest one wins. A shortest interval between pictures (30 ms at 30, the first version) bounded the gap only: 33 pictures a second, 11 % over the budget on every layer.
  - The second around a switch from 30 to 60, measured at 1440p (8 Mbit/s asked, FFmpeg, the last second at 30, the three keyframes and the first pictures at 60, with RTP headers): 1.454 MB at worst, under the server's 1.5 MB per sender; with 33 pictures a second before the switch it was 1.564 MB.
  - A still screen is encoded again once a second, as before. Without new pictures the encoder keeps its rate.
  - Measured at 1440p, 8 Mbit/s asked (FFmpeg, the same settings): a 30 fps source gets 39.3 kB per picture (9.4 Mbit/s) at 30, against 19.4 kB at 60 without repeats (4.65 Mbit/s); a 60 fps source gets 19.4 kB per picture (9.3 Mbit/s). A first version kept the encoder at 60 and repeated the last picture between a 30 fps source's pictures: 25.2 kB per new picture and 14.2 kB per repeat (36 % of the rate), every moving picture softer than at 30; it was dropped for this one.
- **Asked rates for the 60 fps top layer**: 3 Mbit/s at 720p, 6 at 1080p and 8 at 1440p (2.5, 5 and 8 at 30). The asked rates allow for the measured overshoot: a 1440p60 share stays near 10 Mbit/s on its top layer and under the server's 12 Mbit/s per sender with its other layers. A 1440p60 picture therefore gets fewer bits than a 1440p30 one: motion is smooth and fine text slightly softer while it moves.
- **The two lower layers stay at 30 frames per second** and at their budgets (500 and 150 kbit/s), paced by time, not by counting pictures (a source at an uneven rate would otherwise leave them at 12 to 15 a second, or still for a second): a token bucket refilled at 30 a second lets them take the top layer's last picture, at most 30 a second on average, within 34 ms of a new one. Keyframes asked for and a still screen's repeats reach them the same way. They are reopened with the top layer when its rate switches.
- **Unchanged**: VP8 sources stay at 30 (X11, macOS, Windows, Linux without VA-API) until a measurement shows libvpx holds 60. The web client stays at 30: `getDisplayMedia` with libvpx in a tab is not reliable at 60 and the browser chooses the rate under load. The server is unchanged: it checks bytes and packets, not frame rates. A 1440p60 top layer stays within the 10 Mbit/s that `video_upload_mbps` counts for each full-quality stream, so the instance's budget is the same. The packet rate (about 1 200 a second at 1440p60 with its lower layers, at some 1 100 bytes a packet) stays under 3 000.

## Consequences
- Games and video shared from the owner's Linux desktop move at 60 frames per second; watchers decode the same H.264 stream, with twice the pictures.
- A watcher's decoder works twice as hard on a 60 fps share.
- If another GPU overshoots more than AMD's, a 1440p60 share could reach the server's per-sender byte limit and lose packets at its peaks; the asked rates are then lowered for that vendor, as Vesper keys its encoder settings to the driver.
- macOS (VideoToolbox) and Windows (Media Foundation) H.264 paths, when they come, get the same rates after a measurement on their hardware.

## Alternatives
- **60 everywhere, VP8 included**: doubles libvpx's one to two cores at 1440p; not measured, and the web cannot follow.
- **Higher rates at 60 (9 to 12 Mbit/s at 1440p)**: crisper moving text, but the measured overshoot passes the server's per-sender limit; raising that limit and `video_upload_mbps`'s 10 Mbit/s per stream would need its own decision on the host's upload.
- **A frame-rate choice in the interface (30 or 60)**: one more setting nobody asked for; the content decides (a still screen sends few pictures either way).
