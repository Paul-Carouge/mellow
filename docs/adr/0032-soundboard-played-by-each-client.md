# 0032. Soundboard sounds stored per server and played by each client

- Status: Accepted (2026-11-01, within the owner's direction that voice works exactly like Discord)
- Date: 2026-11-01

## Context
ROADMAP stage 16.4 (owner): sounds per server, played to a voice channel, and a join sound per member, like Discord's soundboard and entrance sounds. Voice channels exist (ADR-0030): the server relays each participant's Opus packets and never decodes or mixes audio. Custom emoji (ADR-0028) already set the pattern for per-server media: a permission, limits counted in the insert's transaction, a list in `Guild`, a whole-list update event, sealed files (ADR-0020) served to members only, and audited changes.

Two questions decide the design: where a sound is mixed into what people hear, and which audio files the server accepts.

## Decision
- **Played by each client, not mixed by the server.** A participant asks to play a sound with the gateway frame `voice_play_sound { sound_id }`; the server checks it and sends `VOICE_SOUND_PLAYED { channel_id, user_id, sound_id }` to every session connected to that voice channel, the sender's included. Each client downloads the sound once (`GET /api/v1/sounds/{id}`), keeps it, and plays it on its own output device at its own soundboard volume. A deafened client plays nothing, and a participant someone "muted for me" is not heard through sounds either. This is Discord's behaviour, and the SFU stays forward-only (ADR-0030).
- **Checks on `voice_play_sound`**, all on the server, failures ignored without an answer (the client already greys out what it cannot do): the session is connected to a voice channel; the sound belongs to that channel's server; the member holds `USE_SOUNDBOARD` and `SPEAK` in that channel; the member is not deafened; and at least **2 seconds** passed since the member's last sound. Requests are also capped at 10 per 10 seconds per user before any of these checks, since they read the database. A sound from another server cannot be played (no "external sounds" yet).
- **Permissions.** A new channel-scoped bit, `USE_SOUNDBOARD = 1 << 20`, in `DEFAULT_EVERYONE`; migration 0023 grants it to every existing `@everyone` role, as migration 0022 did for `CONNECT` and `SPEAK`. Adding, editing and deleting sounds needs the existing bit 17, renamed **`MANAGE_EXPRESSIONS`** (Discord's "Manage Expressions"): it now covers emoji and sounds, and its value does not change.
- **Files: canonical PCM WAV only.** The server accepts exactly one format and checks it without any decoder or new dependency: a 44-byte RIFF/WAVE header (PCM, **mono, 48 kHz, 16-bit**), then the samples, nothing else, at most **5.2 seconds** (499 200 bytes of samples). The web client turns any file the browser can decode (MP3, Ogg, WAV, M4A…) into that format itself: the person trims the part to keep and sets its volume, then the client resamples, applies the gain and encodes. Every stored sound is therefore short, small (at most about 488 KB), carries no metadata, and plays in every browser.
- **Storage.** Migration 0023 adds `guild_sounds (id, guild_id, name, emoji, duration_milliseconds, size_bytes, created_at_unix_milliseconds)`. The samples are a sealed file whose id is the sound's id, deleted with the sound, kept by the orphan sweep, and served by `GET /api/v1/sounds/{id}` (`audio/wav`, `nosniff`, cached privately, 120 downloads per minute per user) to members of the sound's server only.
- **Limits.** At most **48 sounds** per server (Discord's highest tier), counted in the insert's transaction. A name is 2 to 32 characters once trimmed, without control characters, and need not be unique (it is never written in messages). The optional `emoji` is one reaction key (ADR-0028): a Unicode emoji, or `:id` of a custom emoji of the same server, checked when written; a deleted custom emoji leaves the sound without a picture, as for reactions.
- **Upload budget.** An upload is charged to its own budget, `sound-upload:{user}`, 10 per 10 minutes, before the body is read; renames and deletions count as guild writes.
- **API** (`/api/v1`): `GET /guilds/{id}/sounds` (members), `POST /guilds/{id}/sounds?name=…&emoji=…` (the WAV as body, `201` with the sound), `PATCH /guilds/{id}/sounds/{sound_id}` `{ "name", "emoji" }`, `DELETE /guilds/{id}/sounds/{sound_id}` (`204`), `GET /sounds/{id}`. `Guild.sounds` carries the list (absent from older servers, read as empty), and `GUILD_SOUNDS_UPDATE { guild_id, sounds }` sends the whole new list to every member after each change. Changes are audited as `sound_create`, `sound_update` and `sound_delete`.
- **Join sound.** Each member may choose one of the server's sounds as their join sound in that server: `PATCH /guilds/{id}/members/@me/join-sound` `{ "sound_id": … | null }`, stored in `guild_members.join_sound_id` (cleared when the sound is deleted). The member reads their choice in `Guild.my_join_sound_id`; their other sessions learn a change from `MY_JOIN_SOUND_UPDATE { guild_id, sound_id }`. Choosing needs `USE_SOUNDBOARD` in the server. When the member joins or moves into a voice channel where they hold `USE_SOUNDBOARD` and `SPEAK`, their `VoiceState` carries `join_sound_id`, and the clients connected to that channel play that sound instead of the usual join chime.

## Consequences
- No audio work on the server, no change to the media path, and no new crate: the server's only audio code is a header check.
- Sounds stay in sync for everyone (each client starts playing on the same event), though a client that never played a sound downloads it first, so its first play may start a little late. Clients may load a server's sounds when they join one of its voice channels.
- Lossless 48 kHz PCM is about four times the size of an MP3 of the same length; 48 sounds of 5.2 s take at most about 23 MB per server. Compressing them (Opus in Ogg) would need an encoder in the browser and a parser on the server; it can come later with item 22.
- Older clients ignore `VOICE_SOUND_PLAYED`, `GUILD_SOUNDS_UPDATE`, `MY_JOIN_SOUND_UPDATE`, `Guild.sounds` and `VoiceState.join_sound_id`, and read the new audit actions as `unknown`.

## Alternatives considered
- **Mixing sounds on the server**, or the sender streaming the sound inside their microphone track. Mixing needs Opus decoding and encoding in the SFU, against ADR-0030. Streaming from the sender ties the sound to their upload quality and to their own processing (noise suppression would cut parts of it), and a muted sender could not play one.
- **Accepting MP3 or Ogg uploads as they are.** Checking their length and contents needs a decoder on the server (a new dependency fed with untrusted files); without one, any file could be stored and every member's browser would decode it.
- **A separate `MANAGE_SOUNDS` bit.** Discord merged emoji, stickers and sounds into one permission; a second bit would add a row to every role editor for no use the owner asked for.
